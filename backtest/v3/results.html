w2: what the Topic Log should rank by

Every trading day at 15:00 ET the backtest rebuilds what the dashboard could see, ranks the topics, and checks the next day: how much the topic is talked about (70% of the target) and how much its price moves (30%). Each direction (social, price, prediction markets, rules) gets its own champion; the champions are then combined.

Result

  • Social (A) is the strongest direction. How far a topic's mentions sit above its own 20-day normal predicts the next day best. X on its own adds to the all-platform figure. 1-day changes are weak, and sentiment changes point the wrong way (on X, big sentiment swings come before less attention).
  • Price (B): trading volume beats the price move. Over 437 trading days and ~1918 stocks a day, volume traded so far vs normal is the best single price input (IC +0.100 with the next move; |24h move| +0.088). The champion uses both.
  • Prediction markets (C) are weak. Open interest and volume vs their own normal beat the 24h changes used so far, but none holds up on the holdout. They stay in w2 with weight 0.05 and keep their role as the G2 gate.
  • Rules (D): median vs dropping missing inputs makes no difference; a cooldown costs a little accuracy but spreads picks over more topics; capping the top 5 at two per asset class costs a lot.
  • w2 = attention 0.35 · attention on X 0.35 · volume 0.125 · momentum 0.125 · open interest vs normal 0.05. It beats w1 by +0.10 IC on the holdout and momentum alone by +0.26.

Is momentum in?

Yes, twice. The price direction tested six versions of it on 437 days: the plain |24h move| ÷ σ is good (IC +0.088), trading volume vs normal is better (+0.100), and w2 uses both with 0.125 each. Momentum alone is also the benchmark every version is compared with: on the holdout w2 beats it by +0.26 IC (95% CI +0.16 to +0.35).

Method and limits

  • Data: Yahoo hourly bars for ~2,000 stocks and every priced topic (2 years); Adanos daily for ~4,500 symbols on Reddit, X, News (90 days, the key's limit); Kalshi hourly candles since February (4.2M rows, before and after the historical cutoff); Kalshi perps since June; Polymarket daily prices.
  • No look-ahead: prices from bars ended by 15:00 ET, Adanos from complete UTC days up to t−1, Kalshi from candles ended by 15:00 ET. Labels start after the pick.
  • Discovery chose every champion and weight; the holdout (Oct 1–22, 15 days) was used once. One-sided t-tests on daily IC with Holm correction inside each direction.
  • The social era is short (43 days). The archive now records every pull, so the holdout grows by a day each day; re-run in mid-November with fresh, unseen data.
  • Attention is persistent: busy topics tend to stay busy. That is what an editor wants to know, and it is why the social inputs say little about price moves.
  • Kalshi coverage per day is thin (~50 topics) and history before the cutoff keeps the 4 most traded markets of the most traded event per series and day.
